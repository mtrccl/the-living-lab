EXPERIMENT 001 / DIGITAL ECOLOGY

A world that keeps living when nobody is watching.

Genesis is a small autonomous artificial ecosystem. Digital creatures move through three habitats, search for food, spend energy, hunt, reproduce and pass their traits to the next generation. No one chooses who survives. You are watching the consequences of a few simple rules unfold over time.

10 / ABOUT THE EXPERIMENT & DATA

What is Genesis?

A rule-based model of artificial life, not a video game driven by a player. Organisms have finite energy, inherited movement speed, vision and metabolic efficiency. Food availability, predators and the costs of different regions affect the population. Offspring can inherit slightly mutated traits.

What are we measuring?

The number of living grazers and predators, total births and deaths, available food, maximum living generation and the mean energy of living organisms. Mean energy is measured in model units, not percent. The creature observer displays the traits of a selected living organism.

How do history and time ranges work?

The dashboard receives live state about every three seconds. A separate host-side observer can record 15-minute samples for longer-term charts. Hour, day, week, month and year controls choose the same historical range for all charts. Long periods group real samples; before archiving starts, historical observations do not exist. Browser-only previews in the first hour are temporary, not a permanent archive.

Scientific limitations

These are simulated organisms, not sentient beings. Environmental selection and measured correlations inside this simplified model do not establish biological evolution in the real world or prove causation. No external species are modeled. This page provides read-only access and cannot change the simulation.

Project

Source code and technical documentation. Experiment design and telemetry are documented in the repository. Public hosting and external access controls are configured separately.
